What happens to your body when you quit smoking

Your body starts repairing itself soon after your last cigarette. This is the timeline, step by step.

20 minutes

Your heart rate and blood pressure drop.

12 hours

Carbon monoxide in your blood drops to normal.

2 days

Your sense of taste and smell start to improve.

3 days

Your bronchial tubes relax and breathing gets easier.

2 weeks

Circulation improves and lung function increases.

3 months

Coughing and shortness of breath decrease.

1 year

Your risk of coronary heart disease is half that of a smoker.

5 years

Your stroke risk approaches that of a non-smoker.

10 years

Your risk of dying from lung cancer is about halved.

Sources: World Health Organization (WHO), CDC. Tar and nicotine figures are average per-cigarette estimates. This information is general and not medical advice.

If you vape

20 minutes

Your heart rate and blood pressure start to come down.

1 day

Nicotine in your blood has dropped to almost nothing.

3 days

Withdrawal is usually at its strongest around now. From here it gets easier.

2 weeks

Throat irritation and the cough some vapers get often start to ease.

4 weeks

Most withdrawal symptoms, like irritability, restlessness and poor sleep, have passed.

3 months

The nicotine receptors in your brain are back to normal levels, so cravings become rare.

Sources: NHS, CDC and Cosgrove et al. (2009). Research on vaping is younger than on smoking, so this timeline sticks to what is well established. General information, not medical advice.
