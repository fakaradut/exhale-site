How to prepare to quit smoking: 5 steps

A few days of preparation makes the first week much easier. These are the five steps the app walks you through before your quit day.

Before your quit day

Step 1

Know your why

Motivation fades in hard moments, so it helps to have your reasons written down before you need them. Health, money, the people around you, your sense of taste, the freedom of not planning your day around nicotine: whatever matters to you counts.

The more specific the reason, the stronger it is. "I want to run with my kids without getting out of breath" works better than "I want to be healthier".

Try this: Write down at least three reasons in the Reasons screen, as specifically as you can.

Step 2

Map your triggers

Most cigarettes are not decisions. They follow cues: the first coffee, the end of a meal, a stressful call, a drink with friends, a break at work. Your brain has linked these moments with nicotine so often that the urge now arrives on its own.

Once you know your cues, they stop catching you off guard. You can plan for each one before your quit day.

Try this: For one day, note every cigarette and what happened just before it.

Step 3

Tell people you trust

Quitting is easier with backup. Pick a quit day that is not in the middle of your most stressful week, and tell a few people about it.

Be specific about what helps: not offering you cigarettes, checking in on day 3, putting up with a shorter temper for a week, going for a walk with you. People usually want to help but don't know how.

Try this: Tell at least one person about your quit day and one concrete way they can help.

Step 4

Plan your replacements

For each trigger you found, decide now what you will do instead. After meals: get up, brush your teeth or take a short walk. With coffee: change where you drink it. Under stress: the breathing exercise in SOS. For your hands and mouth: gum, a drink of water, crunchy vegetables.

It is also worth asking a doctor or pharmacist about nicotine replacement or stop-smoking medicine. Used together with support like this, they can clearly raise your chances.

Try this: Write one replacement next to each of your top three triggers.

Step 5

Clear everything out

On the evening before your quit day, get rid of everything: cigarettes, lighters, ashtrays, the emergency pack in the car or the drawer. Wash the clothes and air out the rooms that smell of smoke.

Not having cigarettes nearby puts a few minutes between you and the urge, and a craving usually passes in those minutes.

Try this: Clear out every cigarette, lighter and ashtray tonight.

The first days

Your first day

Today you don't use nicotine, and that is all you need to get right. Not the whole month, not the whole year: just today.

Keep busy, drink plenty of water and stay away from your strongest triggers for now, especially alcohol. Cravings will come. Each one usually peaks and fades within a few minutes, even when it feels endless.

How a craving works

A craving is like a wave: it builds, peaks and falls away, usually within a few minutes. Giving in doesn't make cravings stop; it teaches your brain that the wave always ends with nicotine.

A simple plan for the next wave is the "4 Ds": Delay for a few minutes, breathe Deeply, Drink a glass of water, Do something else with your hands.

The hardest day

Around day three, nicotine has left your body and withdrawal is often at its strongest: irritability, restlessness, poor sleep, hunger, trouble focusing. This is not a sign that something is wrong. It is your brain adjusting, and it is temporary.

Be gentle with yourself today. Lower your expectations, eat regularly, get some fresh air and go to bed early.
