Great American Smokeout · Thursday, November 19

One day without a cigarette. Here’s how to get through it.

On the third Thursday of November, people across the US put cigarettes down for a day, or use the day to plan their quit. If this is your day, here is what your body does in the first hours, how to handle the cravings and what a single day saves you.

What happens in your body

20 minutes

Your heart rate and blood pressure drop.

12 hours

Carbon monoxide in your blood drops to normal.

2 days

Your sense of taste and smell start to improve.

3 days

Your bronchial tubes relax and breathing gets easier.

2 weeks

Circulation improves and lung function increases.

Sources: World Health Organization (WHO), CDC. Tar and nicotine figures are average per-cigarette estimates. This information is general and not medical advice.

When a craving hits

Cravings usually pass in 3–5 minutes. Let’s beat this one together.

  • Drink a glass of cold water.
  • Take a short 5-minute walk.
  • Chew gum or eat some fruit.
  • Keep your hands busy: write or doodle.
  • Text a friend.
  • Remember why you quit.

Free help

You don’t have to do it alone. Call 1-800-QUIT-NOW (1-800-784-8669) to talk to a trained coach for free, or find tools and texting programs at Smokefree.gov.

Questions

When is the Great American Smokeout 2026?

Thursday, November 19, 2026. It falls on the third Thursday of November every year.

What is the Great American Smokeout?

An annual day, started by the American Cancer Society in 1977, that invites people who smoke to quit for the day, or to use it to make a plan to quit.

What happens in the first 24 hours without a cigarette?

20 minutes: Your heart rate and blood pressure drop. 12 hours: Carbon monoxide in your blood drops to normal.

Where can I get free help to quit in the US?

Call 1-800-QUIT-NOW (1-800-784-8669) or visit Smokefree.gov.
